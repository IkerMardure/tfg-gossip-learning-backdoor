\section{Summary of Contributions}
The transition from centralized Federated Learning to Decentralized Federated Learning (DFL) successfully mitigates the risks of a single point of failure and centralized data monopolies. However, this architectural shift transfers the burden of security entirely onto the network's structural topology. This thesis systematically evaluated the vulnerabilities of DFL environments, demonstrating that a continuous, well-calibrated backdoor attack \cite{bagdasaryan2020how,xie2020dba}can compromise a peer-to-peer network without triggering standard anomaly detection.

The primary contribution of this research is the comprehensive evaluation of how network topology dictates the propagation and survival of localized backdoor attacks. By strategically deploying a targeted threat across diverse architectural constraints, ranging from dense Fully Connected clusters to community-based Stochastic Block Models, this thesis provides empirical proof that the physical layout of a peer-to-peer network is the fundamental bottleneck for adversarial success. 

To enable this structural evaluation, this research formulated and validated a topological boosting heuristic, $\alpha = d+1$. By scaling the malicious parameter updates proportionally to the attacker's local topological degree, the adversary mathematically neutralized the natural dilution caused by neighborhood aggregation. Across all evaluated topologies, this heuristic successfully resolved the Effectiveness versus Stealthiness trade-off. The attacker consistently maintained a Clean Accuracy indistinguishable from the honest majority ($>0.95$), avoiding Catastrophic Forgetting \cite{french1999catastrophic} while effectively exposing the underlying structural weaknesses of the network.

The strategic placement of the adversary across diverse graph structures yielded several critical insights into network resilience:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Density Accelerates Infection:} In a maximally dense Fully Connected topology, the absence of communication bottlenecks allows a continuous attack to easily overpower the network, resulting in a rapid, system-wide compromise.
    \item \textbf{Sparsity Delays Propagation:} Constrained topologies, such as the Ring, naturally throttle the infection. The necessity of sequential, multi-hop communication physically slows the payload's progression, buying the network crucial time.
    \item \textbf{The Hub Vulnerability:} In a Star topology, centralized aggregation acts as an impenetrable shield against attacks originating from the periphery. However, if the central Hub itself is compromised, the topology becomes exceptionally fragile, granting the attacker an unfiltered broadcast channel to instantly infect the entire network.
    \item \textbf{The Practical Optimum (SBM):} Evaluations using the Stochastic Block Model proved that community boundaries act as natural defenses, making it the ideal architecture for real-world deployment. It provides the rapid local convergence of a dense network, while its sparse gateway links act as natural topological firewalls. Even when the attacker controls the gateway node itself, the target community acts as a consensus sink, structurally washing out the malicious parameters and preventing global corruption.
\end{itemize}

Finally, by replicating these dynamics on the NEU-DET industrial dataset \cite{bao2019neudet}, this research confirmed that these vulnerabilities are structurally inherent to the decentralized architecture, independent of the underlying complexity of the training data.

\section{Limitations of the Study}
While this research provides foundational insights into the structural mechanics of backdoor propagation, the experimental methodology required a controlled environment that abstracts certain real-world complexities. The primary limitations of this study are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scale of the Network:} To precisely isolate and observe the step-by-step mathematical dilution of the malicious payload, the experiments were conducted on an 8-node network. While this scale perfectly demonstrates the mechanics of topologies like the Ring and SBM, enterprise-level IoT or edge computing networks may consist of thousands of participating devices, potentially altering the speed of global consensus.
    \item \textbf{Static Graph Architecture:} The evaluated network topologies were strictly static. In real-world peer-to-peer environments, networks are highly dynamic, characterized by churn where nodes frequently disconnect, rejoin, or establish new lateral connections mid-training due to bandwidth or battery constraints.
    \item \textbf{Uniform Aggregation Assumption:} The formulation of the topological boosting heuristic ($\alpha = d+1$) fundamentally relies on the assumption of uniform neighborhood averaging, where honest nodes weight all incoming parameters equally. If the network were to employ dynamic weighting or trust-based aggregation algorithms, the $\alpha$ multiplier would no longer perfectly cancel out the receiver's dilution. In such environments, a successful attack would require the adversary to dynamically estimate and adapt to the specific aggregation weights utilized by their neighbors.
    \item \textbf{IID Data Distribution:} The simulations utilized an Independent and Identically Distributed (IID) partitioning of both the MNIST and NEU-DET datasets. While this controlled for data-driven variance and isolated the topological variables, practical decentralized networks suffer from highly heterogeneous (Non-IID) data distributions across different clients.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Lines of Research}
Building directly upon the findings and limitations of this thesis, several critical avenues for future research emerge in the domain of decentralized AI security:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Evaluating Non-IID Environments:} Future work must investigate how the $\alpha=d+1$ topological boosting heuristic performs under severe data heterogeneity. Research is needed to determine whether Non-IID data distributions \cite{zhao2018federated} act as an additional layer of natural dilution against backdoor attacks, or if the resulting chaotic loss landscape actually makes it easier for an adversary to hide a targeted payload.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic and Evolving Topologies:} Simulating the continuous backdoor attack on dynamic graphs is a crucial next step. Specifically, researchers should explore how community-based topological firewalls behave under network churn. If a gateway node disconnects, and the network dynamically reroutes its connections, it could inadvertently bridge an isolated attacker directly to the broader network.
    \item \textbf{Topology-Aware Defense Mechanisms:} Finally, while this thesis proves that structure alone can mitigate attacks, future systems must develop active defenses. The design of Byzantine-robust aggregation algorithms \cite{blanchard2017machine} tailored specifically for Decentralized Federated Learning, where nodes dynamically adjust their aggregation weights based on the trust or historical variance of their neighbors, remains a highly promising frontier for securing peer-to-peer AI infrastructure.
\end{itemize}
